We first launched Relisten in 2013<Footnote content="Following up on its prior iterations, Daniel's 'Listen to the Dead' and Alec's 'PhishOD'" />. Our goal remains the same – to make the ever-growing corpus of recorded live music as accessible as possible.

Though being present _at_ a concert is irreplaceable, relistening<Footnote content="That's the name of the website!" /> to those magical moments of improvisation is a holistic experience of its own, allowing those present-moments to live on. Bobby understood this when he expressed his desire for the music to last 300 years.

What began as an exclusively Grateful Dead streaming platform now supports over 4,000 artists. Last month, more than 2 million tracks were played on Relisten, totaling over 300,000 hours (13,000 days or the equivalent of ~100,000 Dead shows).

As Relisten has grown, we've put thousands of hours behind the scenes into maintaining and improving it. Most of that work is hidden from view. But we figured we'd catch up to the internet of 2002 to start a blog and share some of those stories.

Today we're launching this new website, replete with a variety of features to make digging into recordings easier<Footnote content="Expanded catalogue, dark mode, and filtering+sorting to name a few" />. In the last few months, we released a major mobile app update and a large Sonos app update. And we grew our artist catalogue roughly twentyfold.

We're working on some major projects that we're really excited to share with you. As always, if you have any interest in contributing to the [Relisten codebase](https://github.com/RelistenNet), or you have some feedback for us, please [send us an email](mailto:team@relisten.net?subject=On%3A%20Relisten) and say hello!

Thank you for helping to shape Relisten for the past thirteen years. Thank you to the bands that permit taping and trading, thank you to the tapers and archivists who put in such altruistic effort, thank you to the platforms<Footnote content="archive.org, the LMA, phish.in, the spreadsheet, etree, dot net, and others" /> that catalogue the music, and thank you to the listener – it's bizarre and wonderful that we all collectively make this happen, right?

— Daniel & Alec
