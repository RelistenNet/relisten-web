We first launched Relisten in 2013<Footnote content="And its prior iteration, 'Listen to the Dead' in 2012" />. Our goal remains the same – to make the ever growing corpus of recorded live music as accessible as possible.

Though being present _at_ a concert is the pinnacle, relistening<Footnote content="That's the name of the website!" /> to those magical moments of improvisation is its own valid, wholly fruitful experience in its own right.

What began as an exclusively Grateful Dead streaming platform now supports over 4,000 artists. Last month, more than 2 million tracks were played on Relisten, totaling over 300,000 hours (12,500 days or the equivalent of ~100,000 Dead shows).

As Relisten has grown, we've put thousands of hours behind the scenes into maintaining and improving it. Most of that work is hidden from view. But we figured we'd catch up to the internet of 2002 to start a blog.

Today we're also launching this new website, replete with a variety of features to make digging into recordings easier. In the last few months, we released a major mobile app update and a large Sonos app update. And we grew our artist catalogue roughly 20 times<Footnote content="We have even more artists coming" />.

We're working on some major projects that we're really excited to share with you. As always, if you have any interest in contributing to the [Relisten codebase](https://github.com/RelistenNet), or you have some feedback for us, please stop by our [Discord](https://relisten.net/discord) and say hello!

Thank you for helping to shape the platform for the past thirteen years. Thank you the bands that permiss taping and trading, thank you the tapers and archivists who put in the wholly altruistic effort, thank you to the platforms<Footnote content="archive.org, phish.in, the spreadsheet, etree, dot net, and others" /> who catalogue the music, and thank you to the listener – it's bizarre and wonderful that we all collectively make this happen, right?

-Daniel & Alec
